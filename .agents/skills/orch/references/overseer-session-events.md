# Overseer session events

The events the watch reports on the overseer's own session. [oversee.md](../workflows/oversee.md) § 4 applies them at every watch event, as it applies [oversee-events.md § Event kinds](oversee-events.md#event-kinds).

## Recovery requests

The watch judges the recorded controller's session, account and context before requesting recovery. An open controller intent holds that judgement. A dead controller takes `request-transfer` with `cause: dead`, using its pending successor or its recorded typed launch fields and handoff. A walled controller or a live controller on its reserve takes `cause: walled`, with the spent account excluded from the preference walk. A `succession` command on the record replaces either transfer with that owner's command. Recovery opens a fresh session, not a native resume.

A pane's usage-limit banner alone proves no wall: the overseer's pane also holds banners it relays from lanes. A current login wall needs no usage confirmation. An affirmative `reserve_only` headroom reading leaves the controller live and permits its independent reserve recovery.

## Recovery outcomes

The event announces recovery before its request. The next duty slice submits it. An admitted transfer is not a confirmed publication: its open intent owns completion. A refused `request-transfer` is the watch duty's failure, reported as `watch-step-failed` with `transfer-refused cause=<key>` on the watch's stderr, and leaves the recovery announced, so the next pass asks again. A failed transfer reports `overseer-relaunch-failed`, or `overseer-capacity-failed` for reserve recovery, on the watch's stderr, as `pane=<pane> attempt=<N> retry=pending|exhausted step=<step>`: `step` is the failed intent's note, else its cause, and `retry=pending` asks once more on the duty's next run. The failed intent stands in the fleet state `orch-runtime state inspect --fleet` shows. An owner command that starts without answering remains unknown until the record moves or its settle bound expires. Recovery makes no further attempt after `retry=exhausted`, after a walled walk that chooses no successor, or after an owner command that fails or stays unanswered, `succession-owner-failed cause=<cause>`: replace the overseer by hand. The watch's other duties keep their own schedule, so lane mail and owner asks are still read.

## Event kinds

- `overseer-dead <pane> window=<window> passes=<N> succession=<on|off> source=<record|rows|process|context|pane> [record=<server>:<pane>|none]` → the recorded controller reads exited for `ORCH_OVERSEER_DEAD_PASSES` passes. `record` is a saved exit over a pane containing shells only; `rows` is its harness's SessionEnd over that same process reading; `process` is the process judge's terminal reading. `context` is a session that cannot take another turn because its context fills the window, or a prompt-too-long failure while its process still runs. `record=` names a record whose typed launch fields cannot supply a successor. `succession=off` announces without automatic recovery. The successor reads the handoff and follows the standing watch log. Read [Recovery requests](#recovery-requests) and [Recovery outcomes](#recovery-outcomes) before treating the announcement as a replacement.
- `overseer-walled <pane> window=<window> passes=<N> succession=<on|off> source=<auth|rows|account|pane>` → the controller cannot take another turn on its account. `auth` is a current login failure; `rows` is an authentication or usage failure its harness wrote. A usage row stands unless the account judge measures below-mark room. `account` is a live controller whose account has zero headroom without `reserve_only`, including a `plan_ended` account. These readings fire at `passes=1`. A `pane` usage wall requires both the banner and a confirming account judgement for `ORCH_OVERSEER_DEAD_PASSES` passes. The payload carries the observed banner or measured account and reset; an unread account supplies no reset. Recovery chooses through the preference walk with the spent account excluded, or calls the recorded succession owner. `succession=off` requests no replacement. A login or ended-plan wall needs the owner to restore access or sign in to another account; a usage reset alone cannot repair it.
- `overseer-context-unmeasured <pane> gap=<reason>` → this overseer's context record, the overseer mailbox's `context.json`, holds no reading, only the `gap` its `lane-mail-check` hook gave at a turn end or a tool call: `transcript-unnamed`, `usage-absent`, `usage-unread`, `home-unnamed`, `home-mismatch`, `session-mismatch`, `pane-unrecorded` or `session-record`. The gap leaves the context mark unjudged ([oversee-events.md § Judgement rules](oversee-events.md#judgement-rules), the overseer's own case). `home-unnamed`, `home-mismatch` and `pane-unrecorded` are a fleet record naming no launch home, another session's, or another pane or none: run `oversee register` from this pane to record both again. `session-record` is a Copilot session record that did not answer: set the account's `statusLine` ([copilot-runtime.md](copilot-runtime.md#account-setup)). A Codex overseer's reading is its rollout under the recorded launch home, `<home>/sessions/<date>/rollout-<time>-<session id>.jsonl`; a rollout holding no `token_count` row yet is `usage-absent`, and the overseer keeps running on a gap: nothing judges its context mark until a reading stands, and no gap stops a lane or a launch. Every other gap is this session's payload, transcript or unread usage line, which no record repairs: use the runtime succession route in [oversee.md § 1](../workflows/oversee.md#controller-start-and-adoption) under [oversee-events.md § Judgement rules](oversee-events.md#judgement-rules), Hand off a lane, the overseer's own case, before the context wall. It goes out on the first long pass the gap stands; still standing on the next, the watch instead sends the owner one `lane-mail notice --to owner` and the fleet log one row, retried each pass until the notice is sent, then neither repeats while that gap stands. The hook also hands the gap to the session at its next tool call. A record naming another pane, a harness other than the fleet record's for this pane, or a session other than the pane's latest SessionStart row's is another session's, drawing neither this line nor `overseer-context-stale`.
- `overseer-context-stale <pane> age=<seconds>` → this overseer's context record is more than an hour old, and a Stop row, which its turn-end hook writes at every turn end, came after it: the hook ran and did not write the record, so its context mark is judged by nothing. A session with no Stop row after the record gets no such line. Use the runtime succession route as for `overseer-context-unmeasured`, and report the hook's own stderr from that turn end. The line repeats every long pass while the record stays stale.
- `overseer-reserve <pane> window=<window> passes=1 succession=<on|off> source=<source>` → the account judge reports positive spendable credits below its launch reserve. The controller stays live while the watch requests its independent reserve recovery under [Recovery requests](#recovery-requests). This reading emits no parallel `overseer-mark`. Refusal or exhaustion keeps lane checks, mail and owner-ask deadlines active. A measured below-mark reading clears the episode; an unread reading does not. A later session failure has its own recovery.
- `overseer-mark <pane> kind=<context|headroom|rate|memory> value=<N> mark=<N> succession=<on|off>` → the runtime's `marks --fleet` judge reaches a mark on the recorded controller, using the current account and context readings and its inherited process memory cap. At a safe point, follow [oversee.md § Succession](../workflows/oversee.md#succession). A record with `succession` runs its owner's command. Otherwise pass the reported kind to `successor --fleet --cwd [DIR] --predecessor [PANE] --mark [KIND]` and compose the transfer under § 1 there. The event repeats every `ORCH_OVERSEER_MARK_REPEAT` passes while the mark stands. An unmeasured judgement leaves the standing mark unknown. `succession=off` opens no automatic successor; install the snapshot and report the manual handoff.
