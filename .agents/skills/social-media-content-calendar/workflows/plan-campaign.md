# Plan a campaign

Read when planning a campaign or preparing its draft handoff.

## Define the brief

- Record the business objective, audience, offer, platforms, date range, time zone, posting frequency and voice.
- Record required and prohibited claims, destinations, link tracking, languages, campaign owner and tracker item.
- Read the consumer's product and brand files. Mark missing facts as questions for the owner.
- Give each proposed post a business purpose.

## Collect source material

- Use native file tools to read supplied material. Use native browser or research tools to check linked sources.
- List product facts, launches, articles, media, brand assets and campaign dates. Record each claim's source, owner and expiry where applicable.
- Record permission for testimonials and third-party assets. Mark unsupported claims and missing assets for review.

## Build calendar slots

- Select themes that serve the objective: education, evidence, product, community, events or conversation.
- Place posts around campaign dates and the requested frequency. Adapt each slot to its platform and audience.
- Give each post a stable identifier. Use it in the calendar, draft file, asset brief and approval reference.
- Keep one calendar row per proposed post:

| Post | Proposed date and time zone | Platform and account | Theme and objective | Owner | Draft and assets | State | Approval record |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| launch-intro | To confirm | Consumer's selected account | Explain the launch | Campaign owner | Local file paths | draft | Pending |

## Write briefs and drafts

- For each slot, record the opening, core message, format, copy length, call to action, destination link, tags and mentions, and success measure.
- Specify asset content, dimensions, captions and accessibility text. Link each factual claim to the claim inventory.
- Draft copy in the consumer's voice. Preserve the campaign identity while adapting wording and format to each platform.
- Use available native media tools when the task calls for assets. If those tools are unavailable, supply an asset brief and mark the asset as missing.
- Record draft copy and asset status for every slot.

## Review each piece

- Check claims, tone, repetition, rights, permission, accessibility, disclosures, link destinations, dates and sensitive events.
- Mark each piece with a state from the skill's Draft states table and an owner. A review issue keeps the piece in `needs review`.
- Include AI media disclosures where the target platform requires them.

## Hand off the approval batch

- Show the publicist the exact copy, assets, destination account and proposed timing for each piece. List unresolved claims and missing assets.
- Link each piece to its tracker item. Leave approval pending until the user's explicit yes for that exact piece is recorded there.
- If the tracker record is unavailable, keep the piece in `needs review`. A calendar entry cannot replace the approval record.
- Hand off the local draft package and calendar. Report the proposed timing as a plan.

## Verify the handoff

- Every slot has an objective, an owner, draft copy and asset status.
- Each claim has evidence or an open review question.
- Each approved piece links to the user's record for that piece.
- The package distinguishes proposed dates from confirmed sending results.
