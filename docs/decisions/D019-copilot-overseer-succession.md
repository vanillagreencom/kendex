## Decision

1. **First entry.** `ORCH_OVERSEER_PREFERENCE` stays the one order for every caller harness, and the caller entry stays last. No script holds a harness-specific order. A consumer that wants a Copilot overseer to stay on Copilot names a `copilot:<model>:<effort>` entry first, for example `copilot:claude-opus-5.5:high`. A successor whose harness differs from the caller's is named on stdout as `successor-harness-changed from=<harness> to=<harness> entry=<entry>` and in one fleet-log row, so the setting conflict is visible after the fact and not only as stderr skip lines.
2. **Pool judgement.** A Copilot account is one monthly credit pool with no model window. `lanes pick` judges it on that pool with `--binding-floor`, read from the stored login, a provider `accounts` row, or `ORCH_LANE_COPILOT_POOL`. An unmeasured pool is never room for a successor pick, with one exception: at the context mark, an entry of the caller's harness keeps the caller's own account when its headroom is unmeasured (`CALLER_STATE` `unmeasured`) and that entry's `lanes pick` finds no account with room, because the successor spends the pool the caller already spends. The exception covers a named entry of the caller's harness, such as item 1's `copilot:<model>:<effort>`, as well as the caller entry. A named entry launches its own model and effort on that account. On main a named entry goes through `ol_pick_lane`, whose exit 3 on an unmeasured pool makes `ol_walk` skip to the next entry, so without this a Copilot-first preference still opens a Claude successor wherever a Claude account has room. The run prints `successor-account-unmeasured lane=<dir> status=<status> detail=<detail>` and writes one fleet-log row. An unreadable judge (`CALLER_STATE` `unreadable`) keeps no account: the entry still goes through `lanes pick`, which can still choose an account, and the run refuses only where that pick fails (`lanes-failed`) or finds nothing (`no-lane-qualifies`). The exception holds for every harness, since it is a rule about the caller's own account. It reverses the never-reused rule main states and tests for every harness. The test outcomes follow from the rule: a context-mark case where another account has room stands, as the control for the no-room condition, and a case where no account qualifies flips to a launch on the caller's own account.
3. **Own seat.** The caller's own account is a valid successor seat at the context mark (measured above the trigger, or unmeasured under item 2) and at a death, where the recorded line names it. It is never a successor seat at the headroom mark, where the account is at its trigger and the pick refuses it. It is never one at the rate mark, the qualifying mark or a wall, which each exclude it from the walk. On a one-account Copilot fleet those four marks find no Copilot successor, and the walk goes on to the other harnesses the preference names, then to item 5. Since KEN-2491, Copilot lanes may also launch on the overseer's own pool account. `ORCH_LANE_MAX_PCT` (95) and `ORCH_OVERSEER_HEADROOM_PCT` (5) meet at the same reading, so lanes stop launching on that pool at the reading where the overseer's headroom mark fires. This record adds no overseer reserve.
4. **First-turn proof.** `ol_session_verify` keeps its three steps inside one `--wait-secs` bound: the early account read, the running-turn wait, and the deciding account read. For a Copilot successor, the running-turn wait also accepts the overseer context record (`context.json` in the overseer mailbox, written by `lane_context_record` in `lib/lane-context.sh`) where its `pane_key` names the successor's pane and its `at` is no earlier than the launch. That record comes from Copilot's `session.usage_info`, which fires at every model call and which the `kendex-lane-context` extension hands to `lane-mail-check usage` ([D015](D015-copilot-compaction-handoff.md) item 1); `ol_walk` runs `copilot_context_install` on the successor's `COPILOT_HOME` before the launch, and a dead-pane replay reuses a home its first launch set up. The hook writes the record only for the session the oversee record names, under that session's own pane key (`gated_box` in `hooks/lane-mail-check.sh`), and `ol_succession` names the successor there before `ol_session_verify` runs. The pane key, not a pre-launch session set, separates caller from successor: once the record names the successor, the caller's readings are not recorded, and its turn end writes only a `pane-unrecorded` gap under its own key. Copilot's session-state files are no part of the proof. A successor that records nothing, because the oversee record does not name it or its hook scope lacks `lane-mail-check`, is proved by its pane alone. The pane reading remains enough for every harness. A `successor-not-working` refusal names its stage: `stage=no-reading` (no record for the successor's pane since the launch) or `stage=pane` (a harness with no context reading). It writes the last screen to a file beside the fleet state and names that path in its fleet-log row. Claude, Codex and Pi successors keep the pane reading alone.
5. **No Copilot seat.** Where no entry qualifies, the caller keeps running, as today. Every refusal of a live succession writes one fleet-log row, the refusals before the launch included, and sends the owner one `lane-mail notice --to owner` naming the mark, and the account and its reset where the mark names one (`mark=headroom`). At the context mark the account has room or was never measured, so naming it would send the owner to wait on a reset that unblocks nothing, as `oversee-succeed`'s `no-lane-qualifies` help states. "Every refusal" means every `die_status` of a succeed-mode run from its mode decision on, for example `no-lane-qualifies`, `copilot-account-unknown` and `model-window-unknown`. A usage or settings refusal before the mode decision writes neither. The walk follows the named preference entries, then the caller entry, which sweeps the caller's own harness. It never opens a harness that is neither named nor the caller's. At the context mark, the overseer's turn-end refusal at the mark ([D015](D015-copilot-compaction-handoff.md) item 3, Judge; `oversee-events.md` § The overseer's own case) holds the turn end until the handoff record stands, and a person starts the successor.
6. **Consumer reach.** A fix reaches a consumer only through that consumer's own refresh. kendex writes nothing into a consumer repository. The fleet watch start runs `kendex check --json --report-only`, the drift check the SessionStart hook `hooks/session-drift-check.sh` already runs as `kendex check --quiet --report-only`; without `--report-only` the check may write a project's `.kendex-lock.json`. The watch start reports `orch-behind`, once per watch start with one owner notice, exactly when the report says the orch skill has a newer version on its source. It reports `orch-currency-unread` whenever the report cannot establish orch's update status: no readable report, the CLI missing included, a line about orch that could not be evaluated, or a report-wide or source-wide failure that names no package. It stays silent otherwise, as for another package's lines or an orch line about mixed versions or links. The check reads the source mirrors offline and refreshes a stale mirror in the background, so `orch-behind` shows a pending update only after the mirror refresh. The report adds no second lock reader or update check. A succession fix item's live acceptance names the consumer whose overseer observes it, and that observation waits for the consumer's lock to record a source commit that contains the fix.

## Rationale

- The preference is the owner's model order. KEN-2496 set the default to Opus, then Sol. A harness-first rule would keep a Codex overseer on Sol at its context mark where Opus has room. It would also put a model order into the scripts, which the comment on `OL_DEFAULT_PREFERENCE` rules out.
- At the context mark the account is not the problem. Refusing a successor on an unmeasured caller account leaves the same session spending the same pool until Copilot compacts and a person hands over by hand. A `--dead-pane` relaunch already replays the recorded account with no measurement. The rule removes kendex#3371 cause 3 for any future login-format change, not only for Copilot CLI 1.0.90's `copilotTokens`.
- The pane reading is the only Copilot first-turn proof today: the model-turn footer is measured on Copilot CLI 1.0.90, but it stays a screen reading. The Copilot SDK documents `session.usage_info` as fired at every model call, and kendex already installs its reader in every Copilot successor's home and records each reading keyed by pane (D015), so the proof reads none of Copilot's internal session files.
- A refusal that only prints to stderr reaches nobody when the overseer is unattended. The fleet log and the owner mailbox reach the owner through the Slack relay (D009, D014).
- vgs removed its automatic refresh, and still refreshed twice within thirteen hours, with no refresh workflow. The SessionStart drift hook already tells each new overseer generation of an outdated orch skill, but it tells the agent only. The watch-start report adds the owner notice, so the owner on the machine that runs the stale render learns of it through the Slack relay.

## Alternatives considered

- **The caller's own harness first.** Rejected: it overrides the owner's ladder for every harness, and a consumer can already state it with one `copilot:` entry.
- **The preference filtered to the caller's harness.** Rejected: it removes the cross-harness move at a wall, which is how a one-account Copilot fleet still finds a successor.
- **Keep unmeasured as never room, even for the caller's account at the context mark.** Rejected: the refusal protects no account, since the caller keeps spending the same pool.
- **A longer `--wait-secs` for Copilot.** Rejected: it delays every refusal and still cannot tell a slow start from a turn the pane never draws.
- **A kendex job that refreshes a consumer.** Rejected: vgs removed its refresh deliberately, and a refresh is the consumer's commit to make.
