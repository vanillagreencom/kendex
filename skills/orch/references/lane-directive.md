# Lane directive

Read the matching section when [oversee.md § Lane directive](../workflows/oversee.md#lane-directive) cannot select or launch a lane. Read Recovery relaunch when [oversee.md](../workflows/oversee.md#recovery-relaunch) recovers a dead, walled or handed-off lane. A runtime refusal's line, `orch-runtime: KEY field=value ...`, and a launch's observation carry the fields; this reference supplies their conditional remedies.

## No lane has room

A launch's account is judged by the runtime's account judge, `orch-runtime accounts pick`, the one judge every launch, turn-end mark and succession pick asks; its reply carries the answer's word and its exit by number: 3 no eligible account (`none`, `walled`, `reserved`, `retired`, or `excluded` for a named excluded account), 4 `no-record`, 5 `unmeasured`, 7 `cloud-repo-unset` and 1 `unjudged`. On a walled answer, read the roster, `orch-runtime accounts roster`, whose `account` lines carry each account's `status=`, `verdict=` and `headroom_pct=`; wait only when its accounts are over the threshold, and report every `expired`, `plan_ended`, `unreachable`, `refused`, `no_credentials`, `no_usage_data` or `error` lane, and every `rate_limited` lane with a null `headroom_pct`, to the operator.

Which of these statuses a failed Codex renewal takes is stated once, in the Codex renewal's table in [runtime/src/ops/accounts/renewal.rs](../runtime/src/ops/accounts/renewal.rs). An `expired` lane is one whose login is proven dead, or a Codex lane whose failed renewal that section lists as `expired`; its `detail` names why, and it is the only one of these an operator answers by logging in again, or by the command its `detail` names. A `plan_ended` lane reads `walled` with no reset, so it is never waited on: its plan ended or its organization disabled subscription access, and only the owner restoring the plan or that access, or taking the account out of the fleet, brings it back.

A `rate_limited` lane met an endpoint answering HTTP 429. A usage refresh refused while the host holds an eligible figure is reported on that figure, with its age in `usage_age_s`, and judged on it like an `ok` lane. The figure must be younger than five hours. A consumed bucket reset ends eligibility before that age limit. A live recorded refusal still blocks a new request after the reset and reports no usable figure, with null `headroom_pct`. A token renewal refused with 429, or a usage 429 with no eligible figure, carries no window. An `unreachable` one met an endpoint answering 5xx, an endpoint it could not reach, or a credentials lock another tool held. Both leave the login untested, so the answer is another lane and never a re-login. A `refused` one met a usage endpoint answering another code outside 2xx, 401 and 403 included; `detail` carries the code, and it is not a re-login either, since the code names no cause the listing judges. Where an endpoint answered, `detail` carries the code and the lane is left alone until the recorded window passes. An `error` lane failed a step on this machine, a renewal's included. Every Claude account `error` for a missing OAuth client id needs `ORCH_LANES_CLAUDE_CLIENT_ID`, set once per host.

Room is judged on the account's plan windows, weekly or monthly or a model's own weekly window, and on its API or Codex credits; the 5-hour window is no wall, and a 5-hour figure alone is no reading. The candidates are the accounts `ORCH_LANE_DIRS` configures ([settings.md](settings.md#orch-lane-dirs)); with none set the roster notes `accounts-unconfigured key=ORCH_LANE_DIRS`, and a configured directory the listing cannot read refuses the read as `account-dir-unreadable path= cause=`. A directory a pick names that no row lists is measured as the pick's billed harness, and a token-signed home nothing lists reads `unmeasured`.

Two more causes of a walled answer are not a reset to wait for. An account the roster shows with room can be counted `walled` on its projection: its `claims`, the fleet's lanes live on it, and the candidate lane are each charged its burn on the binding window up to that window's reset, at least one hour, which carries `projected_headroom_pct` to the threshold ([settings.md](settings.md#orch-lane-burn-pct-per-hour)). API and cloud grants have no in-flight burn. It returns as those live lanes end, so wait for a lane on it to close. A seat, the account this fleet's overseer runs on or a provider's `seat=1` row, is also charged what its overseer spends to the window's reset, one hour where no reset is stated, and the room left is held to `min(ORCH_LANE_MAX_PCT, 100 - ORCH_OVERSEER_HEADROOM_PCT)` ([settings.md](settings.md#orch-overseer-headroom-pct)); a seat with no room there reads `seat-reserve`. A `none` answer's `seats` names each seat it left out with `need`, the room the seat keeps: the headroom its limit leaves plus the overseer's spend to the reset, rounded up to a whole percent. It returns, ranked last, when its projected room rises above that need; wait for that room or launch on another account. A Copilot pool pick, through Copilot or Pi, never takes an account as a seat; its pool reading and monthly claim burn bound the pick.

A Codex account past its plan windows is not walled while its credit balance is above `ORCH_LANE_CODEX_CREDIT_FLOOR`, its reading has credits, and it has reached neither its overage limit nor its spend control: the roster's `account` line and the `account` event carry `verdict=room` and `binding_bucket=credits`. A Codex walled answer that counts such an account walled means one of those failed; the record's `credits` carries the balance and the three flags it was judged on, and `ORCH_LANE_CODEX_CREDIT_FLOOR` is the floor it was held to. Its windows still reopen at `binding_resets_at`, so wait for that reset as for any wall.

A pi pick on a `github-copilot/` model is judged on the Copilot pool: read the judge's reply, `orch-runtime accounts pick --fleet --lane [DIR] --harness pi --native-model [MODEL]`, the hand read of one lane, whose record carries the pool's `monthly_pct`, `headroom_pct` and `measured_through`, `host` for the provider's reading and `stated` for the override. The pool is read live from the lane host's `accounts` row for the Pi root ([lane-host.md](../schemas/lane-host.md), `harness=pi`). `ORCH_LANE_COPILOT_POOL` is the override, judged only for a Pi root no row reads ([settings.md](settings.md#orch-lane-copilot-pool)). An `unmeasured` answer with `refusal.cause=provider-unmeasured` (exit 5) means no read measured any pool: store the seat on the provider, repair the provider's read, whose row's `status` and `detail` `orch-runtime accounts roster --host [HOST]` shows in `result.rows[].record`, or state the override. Never wait it out. An `unjudged` answer with `cause=copilot-pool-unread` (exit 1) is a failed or busy `accounts` read: ask again. A pool the host read that is walled reopens at its `binding_resets_at`, like a Claude window. A walled stated reading moves only when the owner restates it: report it to the operator.

A pi pick on a `pi-claude/` model is a claude pick on the Claude seats and is read as one. A pi pick on an `anthropic/` model reads only the provider's API-credit rows: it spends the grant with the earliest expiry above the shared `ORCH_LANE_CLOUD_CREDIT_FLOOR` ([settings.md](settings.md#orch-lane-cloud-credit-floor)); read `orch-runtime accounts roster --host [HOST]` for that provider reading. A refused API grant has `cause=api-credit` and no retry time. An unread API row names its `detail` and refuses the pick. A local host reads no API credit. An `unmeasured` answer with `refusal.cause=provider-unmeasured` (exit 5) also names a pi model with no provider or another provider, which nothing measures: answer it as `lane-provider-unmeasured` below.

## Lane preference

A repository with a cloud grant lists its Claude cloud entry first and Codex on its own accounts as the fallback. Set the entries in [kendex.settings.toml.example](../kendex.settings.toml.example) § Fleet. A cloud lane declares `land=handoff` and cannot merge. A `disarmed` line from `pr-watch` launches a landing lane on the fleet's lane host through [Recovery relaunch](#recovery-relaunch), with `/orch merge-pr N`. This uses another lane launch and that account's allowance for CI, review and merge.

`ORCH_LANE_PREFERENCE` uses the grammar in [kendex.settings.toml.example](../kendex.settings.toml.example) § Fleet. The launch's walk is `orch-runtime accounts walk --fleet`. It reads the entries in order, classifies each entry's model through `kendex tier-model`, judges the entry's account through the account judge on its harness, host and model, and takes the first entry the judge gives an account. An entry with `@host` is judged under that host, and one without it under the fleet's `ORCH_LANE_HOST`. `--lane [DIR]` judges every entry on one account, a directory or a listed alias, and `--replaces [ITEM_KEY]` on a relaunch leaves that lane's charge out and takes no cloud session. A cloud-session entry is taken only for a Claude launch; its credit floor, grant and repository rules are the judge's ([§ No lane has room](#no-lane-has-room)). An entry is passed over as `entry-permission-unwritable` or `entry-permission-untransferable` where the permission posture of the session the walk is asked for, `--source-harness` and `--source-flags`, cannot carry to its harness. Read `.result.answer`. `lane` names the chosen entry in `.result.chosen`: its `harness`, `host`, `model`, `effort`, `account` and the judge's `record`, which the launch request takes. `none` chose nothing: `.result.skipped` names each entry passed over with the judge's word, `.result.walled` and `.result.unmeasured` count what the judge read, and `.result.unread` names the entries it could not read. An entry the judge reads walled, reserved, retired or excluded is passed over; one it could not read, with no record, unmeasured or with no repository access, is passed over and listed unread. A judge that answered nothing refuses the walk as `account-unjudged`, naming the entry and the step, and an invalid entry refuses `preference-invalid` before any pick. A final entry marked `:last` is a last choice, such as a costlier or reserved seat: before the walk reaches it, each earlier entry the judge passed over is asked again at `--max-pct 100`, and one with room, or any earlier unread entry, holds the marked entry as the answer `held`, `.result.last_choice` naming the marked entry and `.result.room` the earlier one with room. Wait as [§ No lane has room](#no-lane-has-room) says, or repair the readings the unread entries name; a reset does not answer them.

A Codex entry can return an account on its credits, `binding_bucket=credits`, only when no Codex account has plan room: plan allowance resets and credits do not, so the judge ranks every account with plan room first. The walk takes that account as the entry's pick. The credits are already bought, so spending them is no overage.

The launch request carries the walk's `harness`, `model`, `effort` and `account`, and the chosen entry's `host` where it names one other than `local`. A request that names an `account` and no model, an entry that resolves to the harness default included, refuses `launch-model-missing`, and one with no effort `launch-effort-missing` ([§ Launch gate](#launch-gate)): name the model and effort the account is spent on. A request that names neither an account nor a model runs the harness's own default and records none.

## Launch gate

A launch request is judged in one transition before anything is written, then prepared by its effect. The two answer in different places.

The transition's refusals end the `op` with exit 1, `ok: false` and the line `orch-runtime: KEY field=value ...` on stderr, and nothing is recorded. Each has its own answer:

- `request-invalid`: the request does not parse, an `obligation` of another shape among the causes ([§ Obligation lineage](#obligation-lineage)). Write the request again.
- `host-unsupported` (naming `kind` and `surface`): the request's kind is none the runtime launches, `local`, `ssh` or `claude-cloud`, or its surface is not `tmux`. Launch it on a supported host.
- `permission-unrepresentable` (naming `harness` and `permission`): the `permission` word is none of the harness's permission spellings, or a two-word spelling given in part, so no launch line carries it faithfully. Name a spelling the harness takes, or leave `permission` out to run the `ORCH_LAUNCH_PERMISSION` posture.
- `launch-model-missing` and `launch-effort-missing` (naming `harness`, `lane`, the account, and `spellings`, the harness's flag for the choice): a request naming an `account` names no model, or names one and no effort, on a first launch and a relaunch alike. A Pi model carrying its level, `provider/id:level`, names the effort. Name the model and effort the account is judged on.
- `obligation-conflict` (naming `item`): the request's `obligation` differs from the one the record binds. Report the contradiction to root ([§ Obligation lineage](#obligation-lineage)); never rewrite the binding.
- `lane-live` (naming `item` and `status`), `harness-live` (naming `item` and `pid`) and `harness-unreadable` (adding `cause`): the item's record is in flight, or the harness it records or the turn a wake started still runs or cannot be read. A launch never starts a second session beside one: end the lane first ([§ Recovery relaunch](#recovery-relaunch)).
- `account-spent` (naming `item` and `account`): the request's `account` is its own `exclude_account`, the account a wall spent. Choose another.
- `account-walled`, `account-reserved`, `account-retired`, `account-unmeasured` and `account-unjudged` (naming `item`, `account` and what the judge read): a request with `judge_account: true` named an account the judge refuses for its model, each answer read as [§ No lane has room](#no-lane-has-room) reads it. Re-run [oversee.md § Lane directive](../workflows/oversee.md#lane-directive) step 3 for another account, never the same one.
- `overseer-foreign` (naming `item`, `repo`, `overseer` and `route`): a launch's `mail_root`, a relaunch's included, is a checkout of another repository than the fleet's, by git common directory and origin, that `ORCH_CONNECTED_REPOS` does not list. Launch it from that repository's own fleet, or list its repository in this fleet's `ORCH_CONNECTED_REPOS`, as `route` names. `overseer-unjudged` (naming `cause` and `path`; `checkout-root`, `overseer-root`, `origin`, `setting` or `admission-reads-unavailable`): the binding could not be read. Repair what `cause` names; never launch around it.
- `item-foreign` (naming `repo`, the GitHub repository or a Linear item's key prefix, and `route`): a `github` item whose request `repo` is not the checkout's repository, or a `linear` item on the fleet's own repository whose key prefix is not its `LINEAR_TEAM`'s key. File it in its own tracker and follow `route`, [peer-mail.md](peer-mail.md). `item-repo-unresolved` (naming `tracker`, and `team` for `linear`): the checkout's repository or the team's key could not be read. Repair that read.
- `item-ineligible` (naming `item` and `cause`): the item is a container, by its children or the `agent:multi` label outside an in-PR bundle. Launch its unblocked children instead.
- `launch-blocked` (naming `item` and `blockers`) and `launch-blocked-unread` (naming `item` and `read`, the node whose answer the tracker did not give whole or gave with no launch-blocker field): the Ancestor gate of [skill-rules.md § Ancestor gate](skill-rules.md#ancestor-gate) found an open blocker on a `linear` item or a container ancestor, or could not judge one. Merge or complete the blockers, or repair the tracker read; never launch around it.
- `launch-overlap` (naming `item`, `file` and `holder`, the running lane's item), `holder-unreadable` (naming the `holder` and the `read`), `holders-unreadable` (naming the `repo` and the `read`) and `touched-unreadable` (naming the `read`): the file-overlap hold of [oversee.md § 2](../workflows/oversee.md#2-select-work). Launch once that lane stops, name the file in `in_scope` where the brief adds it as an append-only addition, or repair the read the refusal names.
- `cap-reached` and `cap-unreadable`: [§ Caps](#caps).
- `brief-unreadable` (naming `path` and `cause`): the `brief_file` could not be read. Write the brief and pass that path.

The effect's refusals come after the transition recorded the lane `preparing` with its launch intent. The intent settles `failed`, the record reads `stopped`, and the intent's observation carries the refusal's key as `cause` with the values it acted on beside it, a field the refusal itself names `cause` riding as `detail`, or as `detail_cause` where the refusal names a `detail` of its own. Read it in the reply at `.result.effect.report.intent.observation`, or in `orch-runtime state inspect --fleet` among the fleet's intents. Fix what it names, then send the same request again, which relaunches over the stopped record:

- `harness-unknown` (naming `harness`): the request names no harness the runtime launches.
- `cwd-missing` and `home-missing` (naming `path`): the request's `mail_root`, or the home the account or the Pi root names, is no directory. Create or repair it.
- `harness-binary-missing` (naming `program`): the harness's program is neither a file nor on `PATH`. Install it, or name it in `ORCH_HARNESS_BINARIES` as `harness=/absolute/path`.
- `config-unreadable`, `home-path`, `trust-refused`, `home-link` and `home-entry` (naming `harness` and `path`): the launch could not prepare the account home's trust record for the worktree. Repair the home at that path.
- `home-relative` (naming `home`) and `no-context-reader` (naming `file`, `detail`, the reader's failing step, and, where one stands, `detail_cause`, that step's cause): a Copilot home that is not an absolute path, or whose context reader could not be installed, whose pending directory could not be written, or whose `EXTENSIONS` flag is off with no status line writing the session record ([copilot-runtime.md § Account setup](copilot-runtime.md#account-setup)). Repair what `file` names.
- `permission-mismatch` (naming `harness`, `posture` and `word`): the request's `permission` is the harness's bypass word under `ORCH_LAUNCH_PERMISSION=native`. Drop the word, or name a narrower one.
- `tmux-session-unresolved`, and `tmux-session-missing` (naming `session` and `socket`): [§ Tmux session](#tmux-session).
- `session-owned-elsewhere`, or a session store's read cause such as `session-store-unreadable`: a relaunch's bound session belongs to another directory, or the destination home's store could not be read ([§ Recovery relaunch](#recovery-relaunch)). Resending the same request meets the same refusal: repair the store the cause names, or report the session to the operator.
- `marker-item-invalid`, `marker-git-failed`, `marker-unsafe` and the marker's write refusals (naming `item` and `path`): the lane's marker under the worktree's common git directory could not be written. Repair that git directory.
- A `cause` beginning with a harness gate's word, with its fields after it: the gate that admits only a lane that runs with its own compaction off and whose turns its hooks record ([the launch gate](../runtime/src/effects/launch/gate.rs)). `compaction-on` and `pi-settings-unreadable` (naming the `file`): Pi would compact the lane, or its settings file could not be read; set Pi's `compaction.enabled` to `false`, or repair the file. `pi-carrier-unreadable` (naming the `file`): the pi-hooks carrier file could not be read. `unsupported-for-oversee harness=pi reason=no-window-read`: no pi-hooks carrier is installed, or the one installed sends no `context_window`; install the current pi-hooks. `pi-mail-wake-missing`: the selected pi-hooks lists no lane mail wake; repair that install on the lane machine using [pi-runtime.md § Lane mailbox wake](pi-runtime.md#lane-mailbox-wake-pi), then send the item's `launch-lane` request again, as its `retry=launch-lane` says. `unsupported-for-oversee harness=copilot` with `reason=no-context-hooks` (neither the worktree's project hook scope nor the lane's `COPILOT_HOME` holds all three of `lane-mail-check`, `lane-mail-compact` and `lane-mail-start`), `reason=hooks-disabled` (a settings layer of that home or worktree switches them off, as `kendex hooks-off` reads it) or `reason=hooks-unjudged` (that query could not answer): install the hooks through kendex, set the switch false, or repair kendex. `reason=hosted`: a Copilot lane launches locally only. `host-pi-claude-seat`: a hosted Pi lane on a `pi-claude/` model, which the host protocol carries no Claude seat for; launch it locally or on a `github-copilot/` model.

A hosted request's effect refuses before the provider's `create` as `host-request-incomplete`, a request naming no `account` or no `repo`; `host-pane-verb`, a provider whose host declares `pane=verb` and owns the harness's session itself, which the runtime opens no window for; and `kind-unbuilt kind=codex-cloud`. After `create`, a provider's refusal is its own `cause`, and a hosted Pi lane meets the gate above on the host's own files, read through the provider. `host-runtime-unbuilt` (naming `path` and `cause`) is a host whose launch record names no orch runtime the provider's `create` built from the clone's sources. A hosted launch failed `host-create-unknown` may have left the provider's worktree: launch the item again, and the provider's create reuses it, or close the lane through the runtime's close ([lane-reach.md § Lane close](lane-reach.md#lane-close)), which closes the host from the `stopped` record.

A cloud request's effect refuses `cloud-brief-missing` with no `brief_file`, and `cloud-create-refused` with the CLI's first line where it created no session. A cloud lane whose launch is held `cloud-create-unknown`, `worktree-create-unknown` or `worktree-push-unknown` has no process left to answer it: its intent stays open, its observation naming the worktree or the step, and the cloud create's `answer_by`, the time the asking `op` stopped waiting. After that time, and again before relaunching the item, check claude.ai/code for a session on the item's branch. Then settle the lane with the runtime close, `close-owned-resources` on the `terminal` ground (`{"verdict": "completed", "source": "[SOURCE]"}`, or `canceled`) where the item is finished, or on the `no_landing` ground to relaunch it ([§ Recovery relaunch](#recovery-relaunch)): the close supersedes the held launch, its observation naming the worktree or the step, and closes what the lane holds. The close refuses `intent-open` while the launch's own process runs.

## Brief file

A launch's brief is a file. Write it with the harness's file tool under the checkout's `tmp/`, and name its absolute path as the request's `brief_file`. A local or hosted launch closes the brief with the unattended words, `runtime/src/watch/unattended.txt`, on a line of their own, once: a brief that already carries them anywhere is carried as written. The words ride the brief on every harness, never a system-prompt flag, so the brief need not carry them. Keep the file until the launch settles. A local launch holds the composed brief beside its intent, `intents/[INTENT_ID].brief` under the fleet state's directory, and its line reads that file when it runs, as `"$(cat [PATH])"`, so brief newlines never enter terminal input. A hosted launch quotes the composed text inline for the remote shell, since the file is on this machine. A cloud launch sends the brief as the session's description, followed by the cloud session words in place of the unattended words. An apostrophe, a double quote, `$` or a backtick in the brief reaches the harness as written. A launch with no brief opens the harness at its prompt.

The brief opens the lane's workflow, `/orch start [ITEM]` or the tier's verb, in the form its harness takes ([oversee.md § Lane directive](../workflows/oversee.md#lane-directive)). The launch reads the tier the brief states, its verb or its one `item-tier` output line, as the lane record's `tier` and `tier_inputs`.

## Obligation lineage

The root launch or continuity directive supplies the evidenced original obligation and accepted parent mapping. Its nullable four-field object is [workflow-state.md `lanes[].obligation`](../schemas/workflow-state.md#oversee-state): `repo` and `item`, with `parent_repo` and `parent_item` named together or null together. Carry it as the `launch-lane` request's `obligation`: the launch writes it onto a record that binds none, and a request naming none keeps the record's. A request naming another than the record's refuses `obligation-conflict`, and an object missing a key, naming another key or naming one parent field alone refuses `request-invalid`, each before anything is written. A lane launched with no `obligation` reads as unknown lineage. A surface-2 record carries the binding as [oversee.md § Lane record](../workflows/oversee.md#lane-record) writes it. A record without an accepted mapping remains unknown; never reconstruct it from a title, current parent or checkout repository.

Keep the binding through same-item launches, retries, relaunches, wakes, park/resume, account/model recovery and context handoff. Reparenting does not rewrite the original parent. A replacement item needs the predecessor's complete object supplied explicitly as its launch request's `obligation`, or in its surface-2 record; its current item key stays separate. A cross-fleet transfer needs its accepted preserved mapping when no retained record reaches the successor. Report a contradiction to root rather than enriching or rewriting a known binding. A genuinely separate obligation needs a new tracker item or an explicit root disposition.

The close's merge cycle copies the exact lane binding to `cycle.obligation`, with null for unknown lineage. A retained previous cycle stays that delivery's snapshot. The retained record namespace/key plus `launched_at` remains the supported launch identity; changing a native session or account adds no obligation attempt. Fleet derives ready from the original item's first launchable Linear-state event through its existing history reader. Kendex adds no ready timestamp or backfill. Existing `cycle.stages` remains partial observed evidence independently of full-validation counts; missing coverage stays unknown, and replayed snapshots add no work.

## Caps

The launch's transition counts its cap before it writes anything: a `claude-cloud` request against `ORCH_OVERSEER_CLOUD_LANES`, default 10, and every other kind, a cloud session's landing lane included, against `ORCH_OVERSEER_LANES`, default 4. Each count reads its own kind alone.

A lane charges the lane cap while its recorded `launch` or `wake` identity reads live, while it reads `preparing` under an open launch intent, or while an unexpired `reservation` stands; an identity that cannot be read, or a `running` or `preparing` record that names none, charges as unknown. A cloud session names no process: it charges the cloud cap while it reads `running`, while it reads `preparing` under an open launch intent, or while a reservation stands. A `stopped`, `parked` or `done` record charges nothing past a live identity and a standing reservation. The reply's `admission` lists each charge with its `reason`: `live`, `wake`, `preparing`, `reservation`, `unknown`, or `session` for a running cloud session. A relaunch leaves its own record's `launch` and `reservation` out of the count, so it replaces the lane it holds. No cap bounds the lanes on one account: the account judge chooses the account by its headroom.

- `cap-reached` (naming `occupancy`, `cap`, `lanes` and `setting`, the cap reached): launch once a lane that cap counts closes.
- `cap-unreadable` (naming `source` and `path`): a source the count reads failed. Fix what it names; never launch around it.

A request's `over_cap` admits one deliberate launch past its cap, and the lane record keeps the word as `over_cap`. The overseer writes no counting loop: a launch the cap refused is sent again once a lane it counts closes.

## Tmux session

Every lane window opens on the fleet's tmux server, the socket the runtime's own `$TMUX` names or the user's default, in one session: the one `ORCH_TMUX_SESSION` names, else the fleet state's `tmux.session`, which the first confirmed launch records. The record's `window` names it as `SESSION:WINDOW`, the window named for the item. A launch with neither refuses as `tmux-session-unresolved`, and a session the server does not hold as `tmux-session-missing`, naming the `session` and the `socket`: set `ORCH_TMUX_SESSION`, or create that session.

## Recovery relaunch

A relaunch is the item's `launch-lane` request sent again over its standing record, from the checkout the launch ran from. The record must not be in flight, or the request refuses `lane-live`: end the lane first, through its own close-out, [lane-reach.md § Mail the wake cannot deliver](lane-reach.md#mail-the-wake-cannot-deliver), or the runtime close below. A relaunch beside a recorded harness or a woken turn that still runs, or whose identity cannot be read, refuses `harness-live` or `harness-unreadable` before anything is written.

The runtime close retires an attempt and keeps the item's work. Write `{"op": "close-owned-resources", "item": "[ITEM_KEY]", "no_landing": {"reason": "[REASON]", "source": "overseer"}}` with the harness file tool and run `.agents/skills/orch/scripts/orch-runtime op --fleet --effect-exit --request [FILE]`. Exit 0 is a stop confirmed, the record `stopped`; exit 3 a close that failed, its cause in `.result.effect.report.intent.observation`; exit 75 a close still open, which its reconcile settles. It stops the harness only on proof that it is idle or gone, so a working lane fails `not-idle`, and a lane holding an unanswered ask fails `ask-unanswered` whatever it reads; `"interrupt": true` waives the idle proof alone, for a session that is unusable, as after a model switch. An open intent lists what still stands in its observation's `remaining`: a `pane` row marked `unaddressable: true` is a pane the record names with no tmux server, which no read can address, so no reconcile discharges it and the intent stays open. Find the pane by its id with `tmux list-panes -a`, end what it runs by hand, and report the open intent to the operator.

Compose the relaunch from the record: its `item`, `harness` and `mail_root`, a hosted record's `checkout` in place of `mail_root`, its `tracker`, `repo`, `model`, `effort` and `permission`, and its `host` and `kind` together where the replacement runs where the record's lane ran; the `account` the walk or the judge chose under `--replaces [ITEM_KEY]`; `refresh: true` for a refresh lane, since a relaunch without it drops the record's `refresh`; and the continuation as `brief_file`. The relaunch records the `host` and `kind` of the launch it makes, so a request naming neither is a window on this machine, recorded as a local lane, whatever the record named. The confirmed launch writes `harness`, `account`, `model` and `effort` from the request, each null where the request names none, so carry each one the replacement keeps. `tracker`, `repo`, `over_cap`, `permission` and `obligation` left out keep the record's value. A relaunch naming no `permission` keeps the recorded word while its line runs the `ORCH_LAUNCH_PERMISSION` posture, so carry the record's word. A relaunch is bound again by the repository gates, `overseer-foreign` and `item-foreign`, on its `mail_root` and on the request's tracker and repository, else the record's, before anything is written. It is judged neither on the Ancestor gate nor on the file-overlap hold again: the record keeps its `touched`, `in_scope` and `rebase` sets and its `tier` and `tier_inputs`. A record that reads `done` is the item's next phase instead, judged as a first launch over the record's slot, its `tier` read from the new brief.

A local relaunch over a record that binds a session under the same harness and account resumes that session by its id, under the home it was found in, with the brief as its next prompt. The first of these that holds starts the harness fresh on the brief instead: the record binds no session; its harness differs (`harness-switched`); the lane runs Pi, which resumes by a session file the record does not carry (`resume-file-unknown`); the account differs (`account-switched`); the destination home does not hold the session by its id, or holds a Copilot session with no event yet (`session-absent`); the item's handoff record stands with `relaunch` `fresh` or none (`session-retired`). A session whose file records another directory, or a store that cannot be read, fails the launch before any window, whatever the item's handoff record says, as § Launch gate's effect refusals say. A hosted relaunch always starts fresh. A local launch's observation names its `route`, `resume` or `fresh`, and the `route_cause`. A fresh start's context continues through the handoff record, so its brief is the `start` brief, whose [start.md](../workflows/start.md) § 0 continues from the record; a harness switch's brief also carries the transcript sentence of [handoff.md § 2](../workflows/handoff.md#2-launch). The brief carries the continuation either way, so nothing is pasted into the pane after a relaunch.

A parked record holds a fleet slot only while a live identity or a reservation stands ([§ Caps](#caps)), so its relaunch can meet `cap-reached`.
