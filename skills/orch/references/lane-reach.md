# Lane reach

Load from [oversee-lanes.md § Talking to a lane](oversee-lanes.md#talking-to-a-lane) when a wake is refused or a lane shows a harness dialog, and from [oversee.md § 4](../workflows/oversee.md#4-watch-and-advance) when a lane closes. Reaching a lane at the pane is the Pane paste paragraph in oversee-lanes.md.

## Wake refusals

The wake serves a Claude lane and a Pi lane on this machine. A Claude wake asks `orch-runtime lane status --item [ITEM]` and wakes only on `idle`: its `wake-refused reason=WORD` carries that read's `result.state`, and a refused or unreadable reply gives `unjudged`. The status read selects one in-flight fleet record. It reads the provider's status where the host kind declares `status=verb`: `exited` is `exited`, a failed status read is `unjudged` with its cause, and `running` takes the harness as up. For `pane=verb`, it judges the provider's capture with no local pane or foreground read. On the local-pane path it reads the recorded pane and its screen through the watch's classifier, and the pane's foreground process only where no provider answered `running`: on a bare local host a screen reads `idle` only with the harness in the foreground.

The watch and `orch-runtime lane status --item [ITEM]` judge a Pi lane's last session row after the applicable host, pane, process and screen checks: the Stop row the `lane-mail-check` hook writes in the lane's mailbox at each turn end, and the PreToolUse row it writes at the first tool call of each turn (`scripts/lib/session-rows.sh`). That row answers `idle`, `working`, or `walled` for a turn that ended on an error the limit banner rule reads. A Pi lane with no row or unreadable rows reads `unjudged`; its screen supplies no idle fallback. The runtime's close judges a Pi lane by the same rows.

A Codex or Copilot lane, a hosted or cloud lane, and a record not in flight (`status=stopped`, `parked`, `done` or none) take `wake-invalid` before the status read: a Codex or Copilot session is never resumed beside itself, so its mail waits for the lane's next turn or its relaunch, and a lane that ended is relaunched under [lane-directive.md § Recovery relaunch](lane-directive.md#recovery-relaunch). A record whose `wake` names a turn an earlier wake started that still runs under its recorded start refuses `wake-refused reason=wake-running`, and one whose `wake` cannot be read refuses `wake-refused reason=unjudged` with `cause=`, both before the status read. A local Pi wake then goes to the live session through pi-bridge without a status read.

## Mail the wake cannot deliver

Every lane the wake refuses while its session still runs takes this route, whatever its harness: `working`, `wake-running`, `unjudged` from a wake, and the `wake-invalid` of a Codex, Copilot or hosted lane. The mail waits by default, and the lane reads it at the next point the Lane mail rule in [skill-rules.md § Coordination](skill-rules.md#coordination) names, a waiter's return included; a lane idle at its prompt reads nothing until its next turn starts. Before stopping a lane for mail that cannot wait, read [lane-directive.md § Recovery relaunch](lane-directive.md#recovery-relaunch): stop only a lane it can relaunch, and otherwise keep the session, window, record and pending mail. For a lane it can relaunch, end the session by signal, then close and relaunch it, never as a second session beside a running one:

1. Stop the harness. A hosted lane takes `lane-host stop --item [ITEM] --harness [HARNESS]` and needs its `stopped item=[ITEM] processes=[COUNT]` line. A local lane takes `lane_stop_local` from `scripts/lib/lane-state.sh`, which ends the harness and then the turn an earlier wake started, which runs detached outside the pane and may be the turn the wake refused on as `working`. The command below sources and calls it, run from the lane's repository checkout. `[PID]` and `[START]` are the lane record's `launch.pid` and `launch.start`, and `[WAKE_PID]` and `[WAKE_START]` its `wake.pid` and `wake.start`, which `STATE_READ="$(.agents/skills/orch/scripts/orch-runtime --state-dir [OVERSEE_STATE_DIR] state get --fleet /lanes)" && jq -c '.result.value[] | select(.item == "[ITEM]") | {launch, wake}' <<<"$STATE_READ"` prints, each passed as `""` where it prints null. `[PANE_PID]` is the process of the lane's pane, which `tmux display-message -p -t [PANE] '#{pane_pid}'` prints for the record's `launch.pane`, the exact pane id; only where `launch` is null does `[PANE]` take the record's `window`, a name tmux may match by prefix on another session:

   ```bash
   bash -c '. .agents/skills/orch/scripts/lib/lane-state.sh && lane_stop_local "$1" "$2" "$3" "$4" "$5" "$6" && echo "stopped processes=$LANE_STOP_COUNT identity=$LANE_STOP_IDENTITY" || { echo "stop-failed target=$LANE_STOP_TARGET cause=$LANE_STOP_CAUSE pid=$LANE_STOP_PID" >&2; exit 1; }' _ [PANE_PID] [PID] "[START]" [WAKE_PID] "[WAKE_START]" [HARNESS]
   ```

   Status 0 prints `stopped processes=[COUNT] identity=[IDENTITY]`: every `[HARNESS]` process at or below the pid the stop signalled by has exited, and so has the woken turn where the record names a live one. `identity=recorded` is the record's own; `identity=pane` is the harness under `[PANE_PID]`, read because the record named none, or named a harness that has exited or whose pid a later process holds. The stop compares the start in the form the record spells it: the runtime's `ticks:` or `lstart:` start ([workflow-state.md § Oversee state](../schemas/workflow-state.md#oversee-state) `launch`), or the bare `ps -o lstart=` text a wake and a released launch record; a `ticks:` start on a host with no `/proc` is unread, `target=harness` with `start-read-failed`. A lane no pane carries takes `""` as `[PANE_PID]`: its harness is stopped only by the record's `launch`, and `identity=none` is a record naming no live one, no harness this stop can reach; the woken turn is stopped as above. Status 1 prints `stop-failed target=[TARGET] cause=[CAUSE]`: `target=harness` or `target=wake` is the stop that failed and the cause comes from that library's list above `lane_stop_local`, both empty where the library did not load, and the lane or its woken turn may still run. A failed harness stop still stops the woken turn and reports the harness's failure, so the command run again after a `target=wake` failure reaches the turn even though its harness is gone, and reads `target=harness` with `identity-stale` or `identity-unread`.

2. Wait for the lane's `lane-exited` event.

3. Run the runtime's close with `"mode": "keep-sandbox"` under [§ Lane close](#lane-close). It ends a local lane's validation runs, closes the window and records the lane `stopped`, keeping its worktree and a hosted lane's sandbox.

4. Relaunch under [lane-directive.md § Recovery relaunch](lane-directive.md#recovery-relaunch).

The relaunch's brief carries the continuation, so nothing is pasted after it. The stop interrupts any turn in flight, so it is never the default.

## Lane close

The runtime's close is the `close-lane` request. Write it with the harness file tool, then run it:

```json
{"op":"close-lane","item":"[ITEM_KEY]"}
```

```bash
.agents/skills/orch/scripts/orch-runtime --state-dir [OVERSEE_STATE_DIR] op --fleet --request [REQUEST_FILE]
```

`[ITEM_KEY]` is the lane record's `item`, `[REQUEST_FILE]` the request's path and `[OVERSEE_STATE_DIR]` the fleet state's directory. `mode` chooses what the close does: `close`, the default, ends the lane and records `done`; `keep-sandbox` ends its harness, closes its window and records `stopped`, keeping its worktree and a hosted lane's sandbox; `{"park": {...}}` parks a hosted lane's merge wait ([oversee-lanes.md § Parking a merge wait](oversee-lanes.md#parking-a-merge-wait)); `terminal-state` prints the tracker's verdict alone, `completed`, `canceled` or `open`, in `result.state`; and `if-terminal` closes only where that verdict is terminal and refuses `item-open` otherwise. `delivery` names the merge the lane delivered, `{"pr": {"pr": [N], "repo": "[OWNER/REPO]"}}` or a direct push's `{"commit": {"commit": "[SHA]"}}`; the close writes that merge cycle on the lane when it confirms ([oversee-events.md § Direct-push cycle records](oversee-events.md#direct-push-cycle-records)).

The reply's `result.word` says how far the close got: `closed` (the record reads `done`), `stopped`, `parked`, `state` for `terminal-state`, `unconfirmed` (the close's intent stays open, with what still stands in `result.observation`, for a repeat or the operator) or `failed` (nothing moved, the cause in `result.observation`). A record already `done` answers `closed` with nothing done. `result.notes` carries what a step left: `stop-skipped`, `item-files-kept cause=`, `host-absent mail-status=`, `host-kept kind= session=` for a Claude cloud session, which the close never stops, and `cycle-unrecorded cause=`. A refusal exits 1 with `ok: false`, its key in `refusal.key` and the first stderr line `orch-runtime: <key> field=value...`; nothing past the read it names has changed. The close reads before it acts: the lane record (`lane-missing`, `lane-duplicate`, `record-invalid field=mail_root`), the identity, the tracker's verdict, the lane's state as the watch's classifier judges it, and the host's capability line (`host-capabilities-unread`).

A running lane is judged first. An exited lane closes, its item's files kept where its tracker holds the item open (`item-files-kept cause=open`). An idle lane, or one at the rate-limit model-switch dialog, closes where its tracker ended the item, or where its merge cycle stands, the record's `cycle` or the request's `delivery`, and its worktree, the record's `mail_root`, is gone, which closes with the item left open, for an item held open past its merge by a box the overseer checks itself; otherwise it refuses as `lane-live state=idle`. A lane read `gone`, or with no recorded pane, closes on the same two grounds and otherwise refuses as `pane-missing`. A working or asking lane refuses as `lane-live`, and so does an `unjudged` one unless its merge stands, its worktree is gone and a provider host answered both its status and its capture reads. A hosted worktree is read through `lane-host cat`; a read that fails refuses as `worktree-read-failed`, or `lane-host-busy` at the provider's cap. An unreadable lane refuses as `pane-read-failed`. A limit banner below the lane's last turn is passed over only where the account judge, read in process for the record's `account` under the record's `host`, finds room for the record's `model`, over the whole account where it names none, and the banner dates no reset still ahead and names none this host can resolve; every other reading keeps the lane walled, and its `lane-live state=walled` refusal names the reading as `account=` (`room`, `walled`, `unlisted`, `unmeasured`, `read-failed` or `none`) and a reset still ahead as `resets=`.

A hosted harness is stopped through its provider before the close transition. A Copilot lane on a provider that captures its pane gets `C-c` and `/exit` through the provider's input first, so it writes its session totals; otherwise `lane-host stop`, then a wait for the pane to read exited within `ORCH_LANE_CLOSE_SECS`. A stop that fails refuses as `stop-failed` with its cause, at the provider's cap as `lane-host-busy step=stop`, a record naming no harness as `harness-unsupported harness=unknown`, and a pane that outlives the stop as `exit-timeout` ([oversee-events.md](oversee-events.md#event-kinds)). A stop that finds the harness by the item's worktree answers an item whose worktree is gone with `stop-worktree-removed`; a full close then skips the stop and the exit wait as `stop-skipped`, and a keep or a park refuses it as `stop-failed`. An exited lane needs no stop.

The close transition's effect ends a local lane's harness by its launch identity, the record's `launch`, else the harness the pane's foreground shows where the record names none or names one that has exited or whose pid a later process holds; a local Copilot lane gets `C-c` and `/exit` before the signal. Nothing else is typed into the pane. It stops the turn a wake started, by the record's `wake`, and every validation run the lane's local worktree holds, through `dev-validate-run --stop`. On the tracker's terminal verdict it archives and removes the item's files. It closes a hosted lane's sandbox through the provider's `close`, with `--merged` for an item its tracker completed whose merge stands, closes the window, releases the worktree or keeps it with its reason, and moves the record. A step that does not settle leaves the reply `unconfirmed`, the window and record standing, with the step in `result.observation`: `validate: stop-failed` with the runner's exit and stderr, `item_files: failed`, `host_close: refused` with the refused `path`, `failed` or `unanswered`, `wake: live`, or a harness that outlived its grace (`timeout_pid`). End what the observation names, then close again.

A merged hosted item keeps its record `running` until the watch closes it. At a hosted lane's `lane-exited` or `idle-after-return` line, a lane whose item the GitHub duty read merged, on a host whose files the provider serves, is closed with that merge as its `delivery` once its worktree is gone; one whose worktree stands is in its merge close-out or resumed, and is left. A hosted lane its provider's status verb read exited on two passes, its item with no merge, is closed with `keep-sandbox`, so its record stops charging a slot. A tracker quota hold defers such a close to a later pass. Before each close the watch writes its marker, `lane-close.[ITEM]` under its own state directory. A close that ended reports `lane-closed` with the archive line, and a provider close that refused reports `lane-close-refused` with `path=`. Every other answer is neither event: the watch's `watch-step-failed` line carries `lane-close-failed item=[ITEM]` on stderr, with `refused key=[KEY]` and the refusal's fields, or with `exit=[N]` and the first stderr line where no reply printed. The record stays and the next pass asks again, so an `ask-unanswered` or a `mail-read-failed` refusal repeats until the cause the refusal names is fixed, the ask answered or the mailbox repaired. A close that started and gave no answer is `lane-close-unanswered`; its marker stands, and while the record still reads in flight a later pass reports `lane-close-unconfirmed` and leaves the lane to the operator: read the lane record, then close the lane by hand.

The watch's tracker duty closes a parked or stopped record whose tracker item is terminal through the same close transition, after it records the merge's cycle ([oversee-events.md § Event kinds](oversee-events.md#event-kinds) `merged`). Its refusal is reported once per lane record and terminal state, as `lane-close-refused`, the cause on stderr. A parked item with unfinished work, including a Done item with an unchecked box, is not closed on the `merged` of the pull request its record names: the watch prints `parked-merged`, the overseer relaunches the lane, and the resumed lane is closed as a merged hosted item is. Its retained PR identity follows [workflow-state.md](../schemas/workflow-state.md#oversee-state), so an OPEN PR prevents terminal close after a failed recovery create.

A record keeps its `cycle` across a relaunch, so a worktree still standing, a relaunch for more work or a lane inside its merge close-out, keeps an idle lane's `lane-live` refusal.

A lane holding an ask nobody answered refuses as `ask-unanswered`, and a local lane's close answers `failed` with that cause; answer it with `lane-mail send --item [ITEM] --re [ASK_ID] --file [PATH]`, which a lane whose record carries `host` takes with `--root [MAIL_ROOT] --host` under the [oversee.md § Lane record](../workflows/oversee.md#lane-record) mailbox rule, and close again. A mailbox the close cannot read refuses as `mail-read-failed`, or `lane-host-busy step=mail-read` at the provider's cap, bar a full close of a hosted record on a terminal item whose provider's `lane-host list` names no sandbox for it, as its `close --force` leaves it: no session holds an ask there, so the close notes `host-absent mail-status=` and records `done` with no provider stop or close, and a list `lane-host` refuses at its cap refuses as `lane-host-busy step=list`. An exited lane takes neither refusal, and the `stopped` record a `keep-sandbox` close leaves behind takes the ask one while its item is open, its kept sandbox being relaunchable into a session that reads the reply. A full close of a stopped record on a terminal item reads no mailbox. A `preparing` record whose released background launch job still runs has that job stopped first, refusing as `prepare-stop-failed` where it cannot be.

A record written before the launcher recorded the lane's tracker, repository and harness carries none of the three. The close takes each from the record, else from the request: a tracker-identifier item key reads as `linear`, and an `issue-N` key answers no tracker, being the key a GitHub lane and a Linear lane are both given, so such a record refuses as `tracker-read-failed cause=key-ambiguous`. The identity a caller still supplies is `"tracker": "linear"` or `"github"` for an `issue-N` record carrying none, `"repo": "[OWNER/REPO]"` for a GitHub lane and `"harness"` for a hosted lane whose record names none; where the item key does answer the tracker, `tracker` only pins what it says. Each fills only a field the record leaves null or absent and refuses as `record-invalid` where it contradicts one the record carries.

On the surfaces of [oversee.md § 1](../workflows/oversee.md#1-resolve-the-launch-surface), surface 2 uses its native close and then sets its lane record `done`, because the runtime's close stops only a harness its own launch recorded, by its identity, its pane or its provider. Surface 3 continues in the overseer session and has no separate session or lane record to close.

A validation run the close could not end leaves the reply `unconfirmed` with `validate: stop-failed` and a `cause` of `status=[N]` with the runner's stderr, whose `dev-validate-run: stop-failed` line names the run directory, then either the unit ([job-units.md](job-units.md)) and systemctl's own words or the process id and the step that failed. The window and record are unchanged: end that run, then close the lane again.
